Sheet revamp: mockups

For ADR 0126 (proposed). Not artwork and not a final look: boxes, type and rules only, to show what goes where. Each window is 1280 px wide, the size the UX pass looked at; scroll sideways on a narrow screen. The text in brackets in each note says which decision of the ADR the mockup shows.

A. A player character, as the Sheet opens (Sample 08, Wizard 1)

The head band says who the character is and holds the numbers looked at every turn, with hit points editable in place; the condition strip and rests sit under it. The side column is always shown: scores with their saves, skills, senses, proficiencies folded and grouped. The main column is tabbed and opens on Actions. Nothing scrolls before the scores. [Decisions 1, 2, 3, 4]

B. The Spells tab of a level 20 caster (Sample 07, Cleric 20)

Spells by level, each level headed by its slots, which are spent there with a click. One row per spell with what the table needs: casting time, range, duration, concentration or ritual, components. The rest of the text is a click away, and in full under Descriptions. The side column and head band (not repeated here) stay as in A. [Decisions 3, 6]

C. The Features tab (a Fighter 5, as Sample 09)

Features as cards, grouped by where they came from, each with its sheet line and its uses spent in place. A feature with no line of its own that was answered by a choice is written with its answer: "Fighting Style: Defense", "Ability Score Improvement: Strength +2". One with neither is named on an "Also" line, its whole text under Descriptions. [Decision 5]

D. An NPC or legendary creature: a stat block, not a character sheet (an Imp)

One column read the way a printed stat block reads. Armour class, hit points and speed are written once; what is printed is shown beside a number only when the DM changed it (here the hit points). Play state is the narrow column at the right: hit points, legendary action uses, conditions. A legendary creature adds its Legendary Actions block, its uses counted in the side column. [Decision 7]

E. Print and PDF, first page (A4)

The same parts, flattened: the head band, then the side column beside the main column, every tab's content one after another under its tab's name. What is spent is printed as empty boxes to fill in with a pencil, never as the numbers on screen. Descriptions and kept text follow on later pages, as today. [Decision 9]

F. What changes in a narrow window

At least 1100 px

As A: head band, the side column, tabs in the main column.

800 to 1100 px

The head band's numbers wrap to two rows; the side column narrows to 280 px and skills drop the ability abbreviation.

Under 800 px

One column: head band, then the side column's blocks, then the tabs. The phone is this order too.
